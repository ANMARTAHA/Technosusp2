The essentials, at a glance

Not a lecture — just the definitions, rules, and thresholds you actually need to remember.

Core definitions

Suspension
A biphasic system of finely divided insoluble solid dispersed in a liquid vehicle.
Flocculated
Particles cluster loosely, settle fast, redisperse easily.
Deflocculated
Particles settle individually and slowly, but can cake hard.
Thixotropy
Reversible gel↔sol change — gels at rest, flows when shaken.
Ostwald ripening
Temperature cycling shrinks small particles and grows large ones.

Golden rules

  • Always shake before use — it restores uniform dosing.
  • Package in wide-mouth containers with airspace for shaking and pouring.
  • Store in tight containers, away from freezing, heat, and light.
  • Small particles + higher vehicle viscosity = better physical stability.
  • Stable temperature storage avoids caking and Ostwald ripening.

Neonatal cautions

  • Skip preservatives, colorings, flavorings, and alcohol when possible.
  • Alcohol: liver stress, gastric irritation, neurologic depression.
  • Benzyl alcohol: linked to gasping syndrome.
  • Propylene glycol: linked to seizures and stupor in preterm infants.
  • Aromatic Elixir, NF carries 21–23% alcohol — avoid in these patients.

Particle size, by route

RouteRequirement
Ophthalmic≤ 10 μm, or the eye feels pain/discomfort on instillation
TopicalNo grit on skin; finer particles improve coverage and change penetration
InjectableMust pass freely through the needle; needle-shaped crystals favor thixotropy

Why drugs that react with alcohol matter here

Metronidazole (Flagyl) and disulfiram (Antabuse) can cause a severe reaction with alcohol — so alcohol-containing vehicles like Aromatic Elixir, NF must be avoided for patients on these drugs, not only for neonates.
